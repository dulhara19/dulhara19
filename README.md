# Dulhara

Some people use tools to solve problems. I build the tools.

I work on systems that sit at the edge of what's well-understood — where the problem is harder than the available solutions, and the only path forward is to think from first principles. My work spans clinical decision support, emotional AI, organizational intelligence, and autonomous reasoning systems. What connects it all isn't a technology — it's a method: understand the problem deeply, strip it to its core, then build the most precise solution it actually needs.

## Research & Systems

**Athena — Emotional Reasoning Agent**
Most conversational AI is reactive. Athena is not. It models a multi-dimensional ego — identity, competence, social self, values, relationships — that evolves through interaction. It implements computational analogues of psychological defense mechanisms and measures empathy as a quantifiable metric. This is not a chatbot with a persona. It is the first attempt at a grounded, measurable model of AI ego and emotional self-awareness.

**Kidney Care Intelligence Platform**
Late-stage CKD diagnosis is a death sentence deferred — by the time most patients present, dialysis is the only remaining option. This platform changes that calculus: multi-model ensemble classification for early CKD detection, integrated dialysis session management, lab trend analysis, and CT-based structural screening with visual explanations. Built with an explicit awareness that academic CKD models fail on Sri Lanka's CKDu population — so the system was designed to surface that gap honestly, not paper over it.

**Employee Attrition Intelligence — NEXUS ERP**
HR attrition tools fail because they ignore the signals that matter most: interpersonal dynamics, social friction, invisible disengagement. This system synthesizes realistic social scores with temporal drift, combines them with attendance and payroll signals, and runs a hybrid prediction pipeline — rule-based scoring alongside ML — with full SHAP explainability. HR teams get not just a prediction, but a reason.

**Multi-Agentic RAG System — Nexora**
Built entirely without frameworks — pure implementation from first principles — this multi-agent RAG system assists students with timetabling and scheduling. Won 1st place at a university datathon. The "no framework" constraint was deliberate: understand what the system is actually doing, not what an abstraction is hiding.

**Medical Diagnosis via Knowledge Graph Reasoning**
Diagnostic reasoning is not classification. It is inference over a structured world of symptoms, conditions, and causal relationships. This system uses knowledge graph reasoning to diagnose — the way a clinician thinks, not the way a neural network interpolates.

**Customer Pattern Intelligence**
Applied data mining to expose hidden purchasing patterns in transaction data, translating raw behavioral signals into business-actionable insight.

## How I work

I don't start with a model or a framework. I start with the problem.

What is it actually asking? What has been tried and why did it fall short? What would a correct solution look like, and what is the minimum structure needed to get there? Only after those questions are answered does implementation begin.

This is why my systems tend to be unusual. They are not assembled from standard parts. They are built for the specific shape of the problem they solve — which means they work where generic solutions don't.
